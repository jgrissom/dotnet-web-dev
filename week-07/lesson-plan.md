# Week 7 — Lesson Plan

**Topic:** EF Core & SQL Server — `DbContext`, connection strings, migrations, seeding, and pointing a deployed app at the school server
**Session length:** 3h 45m

> The night the data stops belonging to the process. Three moments carry the week: **§3's empty server** (everything described, everything registered, and the database does not exist), **§5's missing `SaveChanges`** (the form works perfectly and saves nothing), and **§6's restart** (the truck is still there). Everything else is the machinery that makes those three land. Tonight also answers a promise made out loud last week — *"one line changes"* — and the honest answer is "more than one, but the shape held," which is worth saying rather than glossing.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this session, students can:

1. Explain why a `static List<T>` isn't storage, and name what a database gives them that it can't.
2. Write a `DbContext` with a `DbSet<T>`, and say what that property means.
3. Put a connection string in **user secrets**, register the context with `AddDbContext` / `UseSqlServer`, and say why the string doesn't go in `appsettings.json`.
4. Name the configuration sources in order, and explain why a deployed app can't read a user secret.
5. Explain the difference between `dotnet ef migrations add` and `dotnet ef database update`, and read a generated migration.
6. Point at a column in a migration and name the week-6 data annotation it came from.
7. Seed rows with `HasData`, and say why seeded rows need explicit `Id` values.
8. Inject a context into a controller and rewrite reads and writes against it.
9. Say what `Add` does, what `SaveChanges` does, and what happens when you forget the second one.

## Instructor notes

- **Students watch the demo; they don't type along.** Same as every week, and enforced the same way: Curbside lives only in the private answer-keys repo, and the lab is a different app. Say it at the start. Tonight it matters more than usual — a room of fourteen people running `dotnet ef database update` against the school server *while you're mid-sentence* is a support queue you cannot clear.
- ⚠️ **Test your connection string before class, then drop the database.** `dotnet ef database drop --force`. If your own string is wrong at 1:10 you lose §3 and §4 both, and there is no way to fake your way forward — everything after that point needs a real database.
- **Use your own database, not a student's and not the lab answer key's.** You drop and recreate it twice tonight.
- **The terminal is the display surface all night, and unlike week 6 you never clear it.** Migration output, generated SQL, and two error messages. Size it for the back row and leave it up.
- ⚠️ **`Invalid object name 'Trucks'` is the most valuable error of the night, and you cannot stage it.** It appears only once a controller actually queries the table, and Curbside's doesn't until §5 — by which point §3 has already created it. So **name it in §3 instead of demonstrating it**: say what it looks like, say it means the table isn't there, and say it nearly always means they skipped `database update`. Thirty seconds of warning is what stops a twenty-minute panic in the lab, where they *will* meet it for real.
- 🎯 **The seventh truck is what makes the week provable.** `Sconnie Sliders` is seeded in §4 and has never existed in `TruckData.cs`, so when §5's reload shows seven cards there is exactly one place it can have come from. Seeding the *same* six would have left you asserting "these come from SQL Server now" with nothing on screen able to contradict you — which is the one thing this course's demos are built to avoid. **Set the experiment up out loud when you seed it**, or the payoff twenty minutes later reads as an accident.
- ⚠️ **Deleting `TruckData.cs` needs a restart, and `dotnet watch` will not tell you that clearly.** It prints `error ENC0033: … requires restarting the application` and **keeps serving the previous build**, still holding the deleted class. Reload without restarting and the page looks fine for the wrong reason. `Ctrl+C`, `dotnet watch`, then reload — same family as week 6's attribute edits.
- 🎯 **§3's substitute is better than the error it replaces.** An empty mssql panel proves more than an exception does: the message would only say *no such table*, while the panel shows there is no database of theirs on that server at all. Open it before the migration, leave it open, and refresh it after `database update` — the same panel going from nothing to two tables is the segment's spine.
- **The migration file is the beat that ties the course together.** `nvarchar(50)` came from a *form validation rule* they wrote last week to stop someone typing a paragraph into a text box. Ask where 50 came from and wait — someone will get it, and it lands far better found than told.
- ⚠️ **Break #2 is the one to protect.** Forgetting `SaveChanges()` is the most common bug of the week and it is completely silent: the form submits, the guard passes, the redirect happens, and the record isn't there. Show it with the mssql panel open so the absence is visible, not asserted. **If §2 or §3 overran, take the time out of §7, not out of this.**
- **Don't teach `async`/`await` tonight.** Everything is synchronous — `ToList()`, `FirstOrDefault()`, `SaveChanges()` — and that is deliberate: it keeps the controller diff from week 6 down to what the promise said it would be. Week 8's scaffolding generates async controllers, and that's the honest moment to introduce it, with the compiler and the generated code both on your side. If someone asks tonight: *"there's an async version of all of these and next week's scaffolding will write them for you."* That's the whole answer.
- **Don't teach change tracking either.** It's rich, surprising, and nothing they type tonight behaves differently for knowing it. The one sentence that earns its place is *"`Add` records an intention; `SaveChanges` does the work,"* which is break #2 and is enough. Save the rest for week 8, where an edit makes it matter.
- **The debugger does not get its slot tonight** — the segment is already full and `Console.WriteLine`-grade evidence (the generated SQL, the mssql panel) covers everything tonight needs. **Week 8 is where it earns its keep**, on a tracked change during an edit, and that's where attach-to-process should be taught explicitly.
- **`Rating` is a `double` and becomes `float` in SQL Server**, which surprises people who expect a column called `double`. Don't volunteer it; if it's noticed, *"that's SQL Server's name for it"* is the whole answer.
- 🔗 **Two promises get collected tonight.** Week 6's *"one line changes"* — answer it honestly in the wrap-up rather than pretending — and week 6's closing *"next week the list becomes a table,"* which is §6. Students notice when a promise is kept and they notice more when one is fudged.
- **The public-repo problem is the *reason* for §2, not a footnote to it.** Open `appsettings.json`, ask who thinks the connection string goes there, and answer *"it would work, and your repo is public, so no."* The whole user-secrets beat lands or fails on asking that question before giving the answer.
- ⚠️ **Don't type a real password on the projector.** Set the secret before class; §2 shows `dotnet user-secrets list` and the `<UserSecretsId>` in the `.csproj`, which is all the room needs to see. This is a change from previous years, where the string was filled into `appsettings.json` live.
- 🎯 **The `dotnet ef dbcontext info` beat is cheap and does a lot of work.** Three runs, no database touched: the secret resolves, `ASPNETCORE_ENVIRONMENT=Production` blanks it, an env var overrides it. That blank line is the entire justification for §7's second `az` command, and it's much more convincing seen than asserted. If §2 is running long this is *not* the thing to cut — cut the `.csproj` walk instead.
- ⚠️ **Some students are on frozen lab PCs that reset on reboot.** Their user secrets do not survive; their repo does. Say it at lab launch and again with the homework: keep the connection string somewhere that isn't that machine, and one `dotnet user-secrets set` restores it. **Screen this early in the session** — have someone on a lab PC set a secret and confirm what happens after a reboot, since a roaming profile would make the whole issue moot and nobody has checked.
- **The lab's checks never touch SQL Server** — they run the app against an in-memory database. Say so at launch, and say what it means: **6/6 does not prove their connection string works.** Their browser proves that. Both matter, and a student who gets 6/6 with a broken connection string and stops there will discover it during the homework instead.
- **The lab's EF packages are pre-installed in the starter**, so nobody is stuck behind NuGet on class wifi. The two `dotnet add package` commands are in the lecture notes and the homework, because their own app needs them — this is the one thing this week the lab doesn't make them do.
- **The lab has 50 minutes and task 1 is a connection string.** Watch for anyone still fighting it at the ten-minute mark and go to them; it is the only task tonight that cannot be unblocked by reading. Everything after it is C#. Three likely stalls, and `dotnet user-secrets list` settles all of them in one command: running `user-secrets` from the wrong folder (it needs `Cryptids.Web`, same as `dotnet ef`), a typo in the key name (`ConnectionString` singular), and **an unquoted value silently chopped at the first `;` by the shell**. That last one is the one to watch for, because `set` prints `Successfully saved` for all three — it validates nothing. If someone is stuck with a connection error that doesn't match the string they believe they typed, have them run `list` before anything else.
- **Their own app may have more places reading the old list than Curbside does.** The home page is the usual suspect — a `var featured = TrailData.All.First(...)` in `Views/Home/Index.cshtml`. The homework answer key rewires a `HomeController` for exactly this reason, and it's worth naming in §5 while `TruckData.cs` is being deleted.
